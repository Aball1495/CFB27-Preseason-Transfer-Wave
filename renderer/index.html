Tier 2 Behavior

Every position fill works in two layers. Tier 1 takes surplus players from teams over their max and hands them to teams under their min — normal, everyday redistribution. Tier 2 exists for teams hoarding so much at one position that Tier 1 alone won't touch all of it — those extra players get spread out across the league even to teams that aren't technically "in need," so hoarding doesn't just sit there forever.

Turning this off makes the tool strictly conservative — it will only ever move a player when another team genuinely needs one. Severe hoarders keep everything above their max forever if nobody else is under their min. This is a real philosophy choice, not just a tuning knob: leave it on if you want the league to actively trend toward balance over time, turn it off if you'd rather the tool never touch a team beyond what's strictly required.

A team only qualifies for Tier 2 once it's over its max by more than this number. Example: with a threshold of 2 and a WR max of 10, a team sitting at 12 WRs is fine (Tier 1 territory), but a team sitting at 14 is now "severe" — Tier 2 forces some of that excess out even if no other team currently needs a WR.

FB/K/P have such tiny natural ranges (1-2 total) that the general threshold above almost never triggers for them — a team would need 4+ punters before Tier 2 even noticed. This separate, smaller threshold exists so a team hoarding just 1 extra fullback/kicker/punter beyond its max can still get forced to release it, rescuing a team stuck at 0.

Tier 2 tries to keep moves realistic — a bench player from a small program shouldn't just teleport onto a national title contender. This caps how much higher-prestige the receiving team can be than the donating team. A player who'd immediately rank in the top 2 at their position on the new team skips this cap entirely (a real, portal-worthy talent upgrade is allowed to jump further than an ordinary depth move).

Other Options

When a player moves teams, this resets their NIL value and current compensation to 0 (and clears their NIL flag). The idea: a "free" player is a safer, easier keep for the CPU's own roster-management logic than one still carrying a big NIL number tied to their old team. We haven't been able to prove this actually changes CPU behavior one way or the other — it's a reasonable-sounding safeguard, not a confirmed fix — so it's left as an optional toggle rather than baked in permanently.

Playbook Awareness

Every team's actual offensive and defensive playbook shifts a handful of position maximums automatically — the numbers on this page are the baseline, and a team's real effective max can be a little higher or lower depending on scheme. You don't set any of this directly; it's read live from each team's playbook every time the tool runs. A few examples of what that looks like in practice:

  • Air Raid / Run and Shoot / Veer and Shoot / Spread (pass-heavy): WR max +1, TE max −1, FB min drops to 0 -- these teams realistically play with more receivers, fewer tight ends, and often no true fullback at all.
  • Power Spread / Spread Option / Pistol / Option (run-heavy): HB max +1, WR max −1 -- more backs to rotate, fewer receivers needed.
  • Base 3-4 / 3-4 Multiple / 3-3-5 / 3-3-5 Tite / 3-2-6 (3-down fronts): DT max −1, LOLB/MLB/ROLB max +1 each -- a 3-4 needs one true nose instead of two full tackles, but a real 4th linebacker.
  • 3-3-5 / 3-3-5 Tite / 3-2-6 specifically (extra-DB packages): CB/FS/SS max +1 each -- these fronts trade a lineman for an extra defensive back.
  • Base 4-3 / 4-2-5 / 4-3 Multiple and Pro Style / West Coast Zone Run / Multiple Offense: no delta at all -- these are the baseline the numbers above are already calibrated against.
